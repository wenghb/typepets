Remember PAWS in Typing Town? A Free Pet Typing Game for Today's Kids

October 22, 2026 · 5 min read

If you went to elementary school in the U.S. in the late 1990s or 2000s, there's a good chance you learned to type with a cartoon cat. PAWS in Typing Town was a staple of school computer labs: a friendly cat, a few letters at a time, and games as a reward for getting through the drills.

Now a lot of those kids are parents, and they go looking for PAWS to share with their own children. Here's what it was, whether you can still use it, and a free modern option built on the same idea.

What PAWS in Typing Town was

PAWS in Typing Town was a cat-themed typing program sold to schools on CD-ROM in the second half of the 1990s. Its roots go back further: MicroType: The Wonderful World of Paws was a typing program for the Apple II, published by South-Western in 1985.

People who used it in school tend to remember the same things:

  • Lessons that introduced a few new keys at a time, with PAWS the cat as the guide.
  • Drills that turned those keys into words, then sentences.
  • Cat-themed games to play once the practice was done.
  • Timed tests that showed how fast you were getting.

It was built for the classroom, and one teacher's blog from the time notes that it was pricey for families to buy for home.

Can you still use PAWS today?

Not easily. PAWS was made for the school computers of its day, and there's no edition for today's devices that we know of. It won't run on a Chromebook, an iPad or in a web browser, which is what most kids type on now. Old copies do turn up online, but they're CD-ROM software from decades ago, and getting them to run on a family computer usually isn't worth the trouble.

Why it worked (and still would)

Strip away the 1990s graphics and PAWS did a few things that good typing teaching still does today:

  • A friendly animal guide. Kids practice longer for a character they like.
  • Small steps. A few new keys at a time, starting from the home row, instead of the whole keyboard at once.
  • Games after practice, not instead of it. The fun part was the reward for doing the drills.
  • Seeing progress. Timed tests let kids watch their speed go up.
  • A routine. It happened every week in computer lab, and short regular practice is what builds typing skill.

A modern, free take on the same idea

Sharing it with your child

  • Tell them about PAWS. Kids love hearing that their parents learned to type with a cartoon cat.
  • Sit with them for the first session. Check that their fingers start on the home row: A S D F for the left hand, J K L ; for the right.
  • Keep it short. 10 to 15 minutes a day is plenty for ages 7 to 12.
  • Praise accuracy, not speed. Speed comes once the right fingers hit the right keys without looking.
